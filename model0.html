Model 0: two cylinders and one leg

Not a cat: the simplest free-floating body that can turn itself. Change its shape and watch the inertia tensor, the angular velocities and the angular momenta, body by body. The total angular momentum stays zero the whole time, yet the body turns.

The model

Three rigid bodies: two identical uniform solid cylinders (mass m = 1.6 kg, radius r = 6 cm, length ℓ = 22 cm) joined at a joint, and a leg, a uniform solid rod (0.3 kg by default, radius 1.2 cm, length 20 cm), hinged under the front cylinder. Nothing outside acts on it. In free fall gravity pulls equally on every kilogram, so it exerts no torque about the centre of mass and only moves the centre of mass, which separates out. We follow the motion about the centre of mass.

The reference frame F is fixed to the back cylinder (body 2), origin at the joint, x̂ along its axis. The shape is four scalars: the bend β (angle between the cylinder axes), the bend direction φ, the twist ψ of the front cylinder about its own axis, and the leg angle α. For each body i, the shape fixes its rotation matrix Ri (its own frame → F) and its centre of mass ci:

R1 = Rot(k̂, β) Rot(x̂, ψ),   k̂ = (0, −sin φ, cos φ),   R2 = 1,   R3 = R1 Rot(ŷ, −α) The front cylinder's axis tilts by β toward cos φ ŷ + sin φ ẑ; the leg swings forward by α about the front cylinder's ŷ axis.

Inertia: from each body to the whole

Each body has an inertia tensor about its own centre of mass, diagonal in its own frame. For a solid cylinder with its symmetry axis along x̂:

Icyl = diag( ½mr2, m(3r2 + ℓ2)/12, m(3r2 + ℓ2)/12 )

A tensor changes with the frame it is written in: in F it is Ii = Ri Ibody,i RiT. Shifting it to the system's centre of mass adds the parallel-axis term, and summing gives the whole body's inertia tensor:

J(s) = Σi [ Ii + mi ( |ri|2 1 − ri riT ) ],   ri = ci − ccm

J is symmetric and positive definite. Its eigenvalues are the principal moments (scalars), and its eigenvectors the principal axes. Watch them move as the body bends. The scalar moment about any axis n̂ is n̂·Jn̂.

Angular momentum

Let Ω be the angular velocity of frame F (the back cylinder), and wi the angular velocity of body i relative to F, set by the shape rates. Body i's angular velocity is ωi = Ω + wi, and its centre of mass moves at vi = Ω × ri + dri/dt relative to the system's centre of mass. The total angular momentum about the centre of mass is

L = Σi [ Iiωi + mi ri × vi ] = JΩ + h,   h = Σi [ Iiwi + mi ri × dri/dt ] spin part I_i ω_i plus orbital part m_i r_i × v_i, for every body

No external torque, so L is constant; starting from rest, L = 0. That is three scalar equations, which fix the three components of Ω:

Ω = −J−1h = −J(s)−1 A(s) ṡ h is linear in the four shape rates ṡ; A(s) is a 3×4 matrix (not a tensor: its columns are vectors, one per shape coordinate)

In the panel, the body-by-body angular momenta Li are not zero, but they always add to 0: when one body turns one way, the others must carry the opposite angular momentum. Total linear momentum P is zero too, so the centre of mass stays put.

Why it can still turn

Integrating dRF/dt = RF[Ω]× gives the orientation. Because Ω depends on the shape as well as on its rate, the turn produced by a change of shape depends on which shape you are in. Going out and back along the same path cancels exactly. Going around a loop in shape space, where the way back differs from the way out, leaves a net rotation. The rotation does not depend on how fast you go, only on the path. That's a geometric phase. Try the experiments. With the leg mass set to 0, the bend-and-sweep loop matches Kane and Scher's exact formula, 2π(A cos b / (A cos2b + B sin2b) − 1) with b = β/2.

Energy is different

Kinetic energy, a scalar, T = Σ ½(ωi·Iiωi + mivi·vi), is not conserved. The joints (the muscles) do work to change the shape, and it drops to zero whenever the shape stops changing. Only momentum, linear and angular, is conserved, because only external forces and torques could change it.

How it is computed

Every quantity on this page is computed from the definitions above in js/model0.js. The body velocities come from exact analytic derivatives of Ri and ci. Ω = −J−1h is solved at every step, and the orientation is integrated with a 4th-order Magnus method that keeps RF an exact rotation. The verification page checks it against:

  • the inertia tensors of the solids, integrated numerically
  • the exact Kane–Scher result and the exact coaxial-twist result
  • an independent measurement of L from the bodies' actual motion
  • 4th-order convergence of the integrator
  • speed independence and reversibility
