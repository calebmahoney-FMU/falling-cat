    Your body

    How to play

    On a phone or tablet: use the controls under the scene. Drag your thumb out from the middle of the bend pad to bend the spine (toward the edge = more bend; down = toward the belly) and circle it around the edge to sweep the bend; let go to straighten. Slide Twist (double-tap to centre). Tap Front legs / Back legs to tuck or spread them, Knees to bend or straighten them, Straighten to reset. The coach at the top tells you what to do next. The key names below are for a keyboard.

    The cat starts upside down and has nothing to push against, so its total angular momentum is zero the whole way down. You can only turn over by changing your body's shape.

    Bend and twist: bend the spine (W), then hold A or D to sweep the bend direction around in a circle. The body rolls the opposite way to your sweep: watch the dial and the feet gauge (both are seen from behind the cat). In model B, at full bend one lap rolls the cat about 150°; at half bend only about 40° (the roll grows roughly with the square of the bend). Pro tip (found by the optimizer): tuck all four legs (Z C) while you sweep, then spread them once you're nearly upright.

    Tuck and turn: tuck the front legs (Z) and twist (E): the light front half turns a lot, the heavy back half a little. Then spread the front legs (X), tuck the back legs (C) and untwist (Q).

    Nose up or down? Once upright, pump to level out: tuck one pair of legs, bend toward your belly (dial at "belly"), swap which legs are tucked, then straighten. Tucking the front legs first tips the nose down; tucking the back legs first tips it up.

    Land with your spine straight and legs spread for full marks. In models C and D, land with straight knees: the more they can fold, the softer the landing.

    Watch the cat: here you're not the cat. Set its starting tilt, pitch and pose and how fast its reflex kicks in, drop it, and see how it lands. The flight log after landing shows how each pair of feet swung toward the ground.

    The physics

    The model is two rigid cylinders joined at the spine (Kane & Scher, 1969), plus four legs treated as point masses. Every instant the simulation solves the vector equation L = Σ [ Iiωi + mi ri × vi ] = 0 for the rotation of the whole body given how you change its shape. Bold italic letters are vectors; Ii is each part's 3×3 inertia tensor (it is a matrix, not a number, because a body is harder to spin about some axes than others).

    Gravity pulls evenly on every part, so it can't turn the cat. It only sets how much time you have. Bending and unbending, or twisting and untwisting, on their own get you nowhere; a net turn needs a loop in shape space, like sweeping the bend direction round, or twisting with different legs tucked on the way out and back.

    The |L| readout (the magnitude of the angular momentum vector) recomputes it independently from the motion of every part. It stays near zero (rounding error only).
